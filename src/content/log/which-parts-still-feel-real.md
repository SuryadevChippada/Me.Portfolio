---
title: which parts still feel real
tagline: on personality dissociation and choosing what stays
date: 2026-08-17
---

There's a strange kind of distance that can grow between who you are and who you feel like you're supposed to be.

You begin adjusting yourself depending on the room. Different people get different versions of you. One version is louder, another quieter, another more confident, another almost invisible. At first, it feels normal. Everyone adapts a little.

But eventually, you can lose track of which version is actually yours.

Personality dissociation, or simply feeling disconnected from your own sense of identity, can feel less dramatic than the word *dissociation* suggests. Sometimes it's just looking back at something you said and thinking, *that didn't feel like me.* Sometimes it's realizing that your personality has slowly become a collection of reactions to other people.

You start observing yourself instead of simply being yourself.

The uncomfortable part is that there may not be one hidden, perfectly authentic personality underneath everything. We are shaped by people, places, memories, expectations, mistakes, and whatever phase of life we happen to be surviving.

Maybe finding yourself isn't about recovering an old version of you.

Maybe it's about noticing which parts still feel real—and consciously choosing what stays.
